Disposal & Reuse

The journey does not end when the balm runs out. What happens next depends on the materials, the remaining residue and the disposal system available.

After Use

So, what happens when the balm is finished? If the packaging is designed for composting, the project proposes placing suitable cardboard or paperboard components into an appropriate composting system after the remaining product has been used.

If a city accepts the relevant organic material through its municipal collection system, that may be another route. As emphasized by the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), successful organic degradation relies heavily on the local waste system available and the precise material breakdown of the consumer packaging.

Reuse & Disposal Considerations

The packaging material matters. Cardboard or paperboard, metal tins, and glass jars do not all share the same end-of-life pathway. To keep these materials out of landfills, consumers must follow key waste management steps:

  • Check the material: Some tubes utilize a PLA (polylactic acid) or bioplastic lining. Research on PLA under industrial composting conditions shows that temperature, moisture, material properties, and processing conditions can strongly affect how quickly it disintegrates. That means a bio-based lining should not automatically be treated as backyard-compostable.
  • Remove as much residue as practical: Large amounts of leftover balm can coat packaging fibres and negatively affect composting conditions.
  • Separate components: A cardboard tube that features a hidden plastic or mixed-material cap must be physically dismantled rather than discarded as one mixed item.
  • Think about porosity: Paperboard naturally absorbs oils. Because of this, reused paper packaging is far better suited to dry storage (like holding paperclips or pins) than wet or liquid secondary uses.

What Makes This Stage More Sustainable?

This final stage is the critical inflection point where earlier design decisions either continue their environmental benefits or create a new waste problem. Composting suitable organic materials successfully returns organic matter to soil. Meanwhile, cleaning and sorting infinitely recyclable materials like aluminum tins and glass jars keeps high-value resources actively circulating in the economy.

The larger goal is to eliminate persistent waste and foster true resource loops, shifting consumer habits away from treating every empty container as something that must simply be thrown away.
