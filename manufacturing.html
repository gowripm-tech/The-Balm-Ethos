Manufacturing & Packaging

Once the materials have been sourced, the next question is simple: how do they become the finished product—and how can the packaging stay functional as well as sustainable?

From Ingredients to Balm

The goal is to turn selected materials into a finished lip product while keeping the manufacturing process as thoughtful as the ingredients themselves. And because this is a blog—not a science textbook—here’s a little break from the facts.

Why did the single-use plastic bottle go to therapy?
Because it felt empty inside, but refused to bottle up its emotions—unlike the sustainable packaging next to it, which was completely open, biodegradable and ready to decompose without making a scene.

Plastic packaging is everywhere, from everyday products to delivery boxes and even toothbrushes. That raises the bigger question: what can be done differently?

Six-Step Production Process

01 · InspectRaw materials are checked and relevant certifications are verified before production.
02 · MeasureWaxes, butters, and oils are measured according to the product formula, with their different melting behaviours considered.
03 · MeltIngredients are melted using a low-temperature heating system to help protect heat-sensitive components.
04 · RegulateThe mixture is cooled toward solidification before heat-sensitive ingredients such as essential oils, botanical tints, and antioxidants are blended in.
05 · PourThe warm liquid balm is poured into containers such as biodegradable cardboard push-up tubes or recyclable aluminium tins.
06 · CoolFilled containers are cooled under controlled conditions to help prevent cracking and preserve texture.

Packaging Considerations

Eco-friendly packaging still needs to work. Examples highlighted in the project include biodegradable paper tubes, recyclable aluminium tins, and post-consumer recycled (PCR) plastics. Packaging decisions also involve product stability, labels, adhesives, outer materials, and the size of boxes used for shipping.

The material has to suit the product. Plant-based balms can contain substantial amounts of liquid oils, so a suitable barrier may be needed to prevent oil from staining or weakening paper packaging. As detailed in research published via SciOpen, applying bio-based oil repellents (such as polysaccharides or plant lipids) forms a dense film on paperboard, successfully preventing natural oils from penetrating the structural fibres.

Adhesives matter too: water-based or natural adhesives can support a more sustainable packaging system than conventional plastic-based adhesives. For instance, brands looking to implement zero-waste designs often utilize specialized, plant-based tubes like Nutley’s Plastic Free Cardboard Cosmetics Tube, which rely strictly on plant-based glues and interior wax linings to keep the entire container fully home-compostable.

What Makes This Stage More Sustainable?

The project highlights manufacturing conditions designed to reduce water and energy consumption. According to industrial case studies documented by UL Prospector, cosmetic factories are actively transitioning to “Waterloop” models. This describes a closed-loop water system in which water can be treated, circulated, and reused for cooling and heating, reducing unnecessary water waste. During cleaning, biodegradable cleaning agents can be used instead of harsher sanitation products to reduce the potential for chemical pollution.
