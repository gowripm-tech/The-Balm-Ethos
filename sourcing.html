Sourcing Raw Materials

Before a lip balm ever reaches a shelf, it begins with a question: where do its ingredients come from, and what happens along the way?

Why Sourcing Matters

Many of us use lip balm every day without thinking about what goes into it. Eco-friendly formulations often focus on natural materials, replacing some petroleum-based components with botanical alternatives. A study published through the National Institutes of Health (NIH) highlights how selecting renewable feedstocks—such as shea butter and candelilla wax—can support more sustainable cosmetic formulations. The choices made at this first stage can influence the rest of the product’s environmental story.

Plant-Based Ingredients

Waxes, butters, oils, and natural aromas or colours each play a different role in a balm. Butters help create a smooth, moisturising feel; oils can provide a glossy finish; aromas provide scent; and waxes help give the balm its structure.

Ingredients Highlighted in This Project:
Waxes: Candelilla wax, Beeswax
Butters: Cold-pressed shea butter, Cocoa butter, Mango butter
Oils: Coconut oil, Jojoba oil, Sweet almond oil, Castor oil
Aromas / Colours: Organic essential oils, Botanical powders (such as beetroot or mineral-based mica)

Environmental Considerations

Sustainable sourcing is not simply about choosing a natural ingredient. The wider supply chain matters too. Research published by Personal Care Insights notes that poorly managed sourcing and overharvesting of exotic natural botanicals can threaten vulnerable plant populations. When unregulated, these practices can contribute to biodiversity loss, deforestation, water depletion, transport emissions, and wider environmental pressures associated with harvesting.

The aim is to understand those trade-offs so that environmental choices are connected to the whole supply chain rather than just one ingredient.

What Makes This Stage More Sustainable?

One approach is to work more directly with farming communities, supporting local producers and increasing transparency in the supply chain. The project also highlights trusted global certifications, such as the USDA Organic standard, as a crucial indicator used to communicate strict production requirements directly to consumers.
